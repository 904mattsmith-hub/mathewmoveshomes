01

The idea, in one breath

Local businesses want to reach families and homeowners and be seen supporting the community. We give them both — at a price a small business can say yes to on the spot.

We assemble a small group of community partners — local businesses that put in $250 or $500 apiece. That pool funds real advertising placements at neighborhood schools and churches: stadium and gym banners, sports and event programs, yearbook and bulletin ads, festival booths, spirit nights. Every placement carries the Mathew Moves Homes brand alongside the partners' names, so the whole group gets in front of thousands of local families for a fraction of what any of them would pay alone.

For us it is community-first marketing that keeps our name in front of the exact people who buy and sell homes. For the partner it is affordable, feel-good exposure. For the school or church it is real support. Everybody wins, and the ask is small enough that "yes" is easy.

Say it this wayThese are sponsorships / community partnerships, not charitable "donations." We are a for-profit real estate business, so a partner's payment is a marketing/advertising expense for them — not tax-deductible as a charitable gift. Using the right word up front keeps everyone out of trouble and sets honest expectations.
02

How the money moves

Four steps, start to finish. Keep it this simple when you explain it.

Step 1

Partners sign on

10–15 local businesses commit $250 or $500 for the season and get co-branding on everything we place.

Step 2

We pick placements

We choose the schools and churches with the best reach for the budget — banners, programs, bulletins, events.

Step 3

We design & place

One clean co-branded design carries our brand plus the partners' logos. We handle art, payment to the school/church, and delivery.

Step 4

We report back

Every partner gets photos of their logo in the wild and a one-page recap of where it ran and who it reached.

06

The pitch — what to actually say

Short, warm, and specific. Ask in person or by phone first; email is the follow-up, not the opener.

The 30-second ask

"Hey [Name] — Khristi and I are putting together a small group of local businesses to sponsor a few schools and churches this year: banners at the games, ads in the bulletins, booths at the festivals. It's $250 or $500 for the season, your name goes on everything we run, and we send you photos of it out in the community. We're keeping it to about a dozen partners. Can I count you in?"

If "what do I get for it?" → "Your logo in front of thousands of local families all season, a spot on our partners page, a social shout-out from both of us, and the goodwill of visibly backing local schools. It's the cheapest, best-feeling advertising you'll buy all year."

If "let me think about it" → "Totally — I'll text you the one-pager and the sign-up. We lock the group in [date] so the art gets done before the season. Can I check back Friday?"

If "is this a donation / tax write-off?" → "It's a sponsorship, so for you it's an advertising expense, not a charitable donation — talk to your accountant, but most businesses just write it off as marketing."

The one-pagerMake a single printed/PDF leaf: the idea in two lines, the two tiers, the list of schools/churches you're targeting, a photo of a banner or booth, and both your contact blocks. That plus this page's partner form is your whole kit. (Ask if you'd like this built next.)

08

Money handling & the rules that matter

Do this part right and the whole program is clean and repeatable. Skim it now; don't skip it.

Brokerage first

  • Get written OK from United Real Estate Gallery before you collect a dollar — any advertising with your names must follow their policy
  • Confirm how partner funds should flow (your own marketing account vs. through the brokerage)
  • Ask whether the broker wants to co-brand or stay off it

Every placement must carry the brokerage

  • Florida rules require the brokerage name on your advertising — "United Real Estate Gallery" appears on banners, ads, and bulletins
  • Don't imply the school or church endorses you — you're a sponsor, not a partner of theirs
  • Keep licenses (SL3377387 / SL3401775) on materials where required

RESPA — the big one

  • Lenders, title, and warranty companies can be partners, but their money must buy real advertising at fair market value
  • Never tie their sponsorship to sending or receiving referrals — that's a RESPA Section 8 violation
  • Same dollar/benefit for them as any other partner; document it

Clean bookkeeping

  • One simple written agreement per partner (the form below)
  • Receipt for every payment; label it "sponsorship," never "donation"
  • Track money in vs. money spent on placements; keep the two matched
  • Save every invoice from schools/churches
Not legal or tax adviceThese are the guardrails to raise with your broker and, for anything involving lenders/title, a quick check with your broker's compliance contact or an attorney. A five-minute call now prevents a real headache later.

10

The first 30 days

Bias to action. The goal for month one is signed partners and money in the account — placements follow.

Week 1

Set up. Get brokerage sign-off. Build your 50-name prospect list & tracker. Finalize the two tiers and the partner form. Draft the one-pager. Call your 3 warmest contacts each — get the first yeses on the board for momentum.

Week 2

Work the warm list. 5 asks each. Confirm real placement prices with 2–3 schools and 2 churches so you can name them in the pitch. Post a "we're building a community partner group" teaser on social.

Week 3

Push to the goal. 5 asks each, chase the maybes, collect payments as yeses come in. Aim to be at 8+ partners. Start the co-branded design with the logos you have.

Week 4

Close & launch. Fill to ~12 partners, lock the group, and book the first placements the pool covers. Publish the Community Partners page, do the launch social posts, and schedule the first photo recap.

This week — do these three things1) Text your broker to confirm the OK and how funds should flow. 2) Each write your 25 names. 3) Make three asks each, today, to the people most likely to say yes — nothing builds this faster than telling the next prospect "we already have five partners signed."
